Quick Facts

  • Energy labels and star ratings help consumers compare appliance energy efficiency.

Understanding TV Energy Use in Australia

How do TV technology, screen size and brand affect energy use? Explore the Australian TV market to discover which TVs are most common and what factors may influence their energy consumption.

Explore the TV Market

First, let's look at the types of TVs and screen sizes available in the dataset.

1. What type of TV screen technologies are currently available in Australia and which are the most frequent?

The TV screen technologies available in the dataset are LCD (LED), LCD and OLED. LCD (LED) is by far the most frequent technology, accounting for 83.03% of the TVs, followed by LCD at 10.58% and OLED at 6.39%.

2. What screen sizes are available and which are most frequent?

The dataset includes TV sizes from 24 to 85 inches. The most common size is 65 inches with 773 models, followed by 55 inches with 762 models and 75 inches with 629 models.

Compare Brands

The number of TV models also varies between brands. This helps us see which brands have the widest range of models in the dataset.

3. Which brands have the largest number of models?

KOGAN has the largest number of models with 809, followed by LG with 723 and Samsung Electronics with 699 models.

Explore Energy Consumption

Now let's explore how screen technology and screen size are related to TV power consumption.

4. Which type of screen technology consumes the least amount of power?

Based on median average-mode power, LCD TVs consume the least power at 71.35. LCD (LED) has a median of 106, while OLED has the highest median at 112.70.

5. What is the relationship between screen size and power use?

The scatter plot shows a positive relationship between screen size and power consumption. In general, larger TVs use more power, although some variation exists between TVs of similar sizes.

Optional: Are there differences in power consumption between brands?

Power consumption varies between brands. Among the brands shown, SPARK ELECTRONICS has the highest average power consumption at 233.45, while TAVICE has the lowest at 130.03.

Explore Energy Ratings

Finally, let's look at whether screen size is related to the energy star rating of a TV.

6. What is the relationship between star rating and screen size?

There is no strong obvious relationship between screen size and star rating. TVs of different sizes can have both high and low star ratings.

What Can We Learn?

The data shows that LCD (LED) TVs are the most common, while 65-inch TVs are among the most frequent sizes. Larger TVs generally use more power, while screen size does not appear to strongly determine star rating.

These findings can help consumers consider screen size, TV technology and energy consumption when choosing a television.

Energy-Saving Tips

  • Use the television's energy-saving mode where available.
  • Avoid leaving the television on when it is not being watched.
  • Consider energy efficiency when choosing a new television.

About This Website

This website was developed as part of COS30045 Data Visualisation at Swinburne University of Technology Sarawak.

Our Purpose

The purpose of this website is to help users understand appliance energy consumption in the Australian market.

Data visualisations are used to make information about television screen technologies, screen sizes, brands, power consumption and energy ratings easier to explore and understand.

Data Source

The television data used in this website comes from Australian Government appliance energy rating data.

The data was processed and explored using KNIME before being presented through visualisations on this website.
